1Skewness: the idea

Skewness is the degree of asymmetry of a distribution.

ShapeTailTypical order*
SymmetricBalancedMean = Median = Mode
PositiveLonger right tailMean > Median > Mode
NegativeLonger left tailMean < Median < Mode

*For many unimodal distributions; not a universal rule.

2Karl Pearson's coefficient

Using the mode$$S_k = \frac{\text{Mean} - \text{Mode}}{\text{SD}}$$
Using the median$$S_k = \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\text{SD}}$$

Based on the mean, the mode (or median) and the standard deviation. Sign gives the direction: $+$ positive, $-$ negative, $0$ symmetric.

3Bowley's (quartile) measure

Based on quartiles$$S_Q = \frac{Q_3 - 2Q_2 + Q_1}{Q_3 - Q_1}$$

Equivalent: $\dfrac{(Q_3-Q_2)-(Q_2-Q_1)}{(Q_3-Q_2)+(Q_2-Q_1)}$. Lies between −1 and +1; usable with open-end classes.

4Kelley's (percentile) measure

Based on $P_{10}, P_{50}, P_{90}$$$S_P = \frac{(P_{90} - P_{50}) - (P_{50} - P_{10})}{(P_{90} - P_{50}) + (P_{50} - P_{10})}$$

Uses more of the distribution than Bowley's middle 50%.

6Moment skewness

Size only$$\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3^2}{\mu_2^3}$$
Size and direction$$\gamma_1 = \frac{\mu_3}{\mu_2^{3/2}}$$

$\gamma_1 = 0$ means zero third-moment skewness; it does not prove symmetry.

7Kurtosis

Based on the fourth standardised central moment; describes tail behaviour and extreme observations.

Kurtosis$$\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}$$
Excess kurtosis$$\gamma_2 = \beta_2 - 3$$
Type$\beta_2$$\gamma_2$Example
Mesokurtic= 3= 0Normal distribution
Leptokurtic> 3> 0Heavier tails, sharper peak
Platykurtic< 3< 0Lighter tails, flatter

!Common mistakes

  • $\beta_1$ has no sign; use $\gamma_1$ for direction.
  • The skewed mean–median–mode order is typical, not guaranteed.
  • "Positive skew" means the long tail is on the right.
