1What is ANCOVA?

ANCOVA combines analysis of variance with regression adjustment on a covariate: a measured explanatory variable.

Agri examples: initial plant stand, initial weight of animals, pre-treatment pest count, previous-season yield.

A covariate must be related to the response and not caused by the treatment. Ideally measured before treatment (pre-treatment covariate).

2Blocking vs ANCOVA

BlockingANCOVA
WhenBefore the experiment (layout)During analysis
HandlesPredictable variation (soil, gradient)Variation in a measured covariate
AimBoth reduce experimental error

Precision may improve but is not guaranteed.

3Model & key quantities (CRD)

Common-slope model$$Y_{ij} = \mu + \tau_i + \beta(X_{ij} - \bar{X}) + e_{ij}$$
Slope$$\hat\beta = \frac{E_{xy}}{E_{xx}}$$
Adjusted error SS$$E = E_{yy} - \frac{E_{xy}^2}{E_{xx}}$$
Adjusted MSE$$MSE_{adj} = \frac{E}{n - v - 1}$$

$E_{xx}, E_{xy}, E_{yy}$ = within-treatment (error) sums of squares and products. RBD error df: $(r-1)(v-1) - 1$.

4Degrees of freedom

Fitting one covariate slope uses one error df.

CRD$n - v - 1$
RBD$(r-1)(v-1) - 1$

5Adjusted means & F test

Adjusted treatment mean$$\bar{Y}_{i,adj} = \bar{Y}_i - \hat\beta(\bar{X}_i - \bar{X})$$
Adjusted F$$F = \frac{(E_1 - E)/(v - 1)}{MSE_{adj}}$$

$E_1$: residual SS of the reduced model (no treatments, covariate kept); $E$: full model. Means are compared at a common covariate value.

6Assumptions

  • The usual ANOVA assumptions: independent errors, common variance, normality for exact tests
  • Linear relationship between response and covariate
  • Equal slopes across treatments (check treatment × covariate interaction)
  • Covariate not affected by the treatments
  • Error mean is zero

7Steps of the analysis

  1. Compute SS for $X$, $Y$ and SP for $XY$
  2. Get error $E_{xx}, E_{xy}, E_{yy}$
  3. Estimate $\hat\beta = E_{xy}/E_{xx}$
  4. Adjusted error SS and $MSE_{adj}$
  5. Adjusted treatment SS: $E_1 - E$
  6. Adjusted $F$ test
  7. Compute adjusted means
  8. Compare with pair-specific SEs

!Common mistakes

  • Using a covariate the treatment affects.
  • Forgetting the extra df lost.
  • Assuming adjustment turns observational data into an experiment.
