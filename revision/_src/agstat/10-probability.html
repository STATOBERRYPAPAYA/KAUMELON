1Basic terms

Random experimentOutcome cannot be predicted with certainty
Sample space $S$Set of all possible outcomes
Sample pointOne possible outcome
EventA subset of the sample space
Random variableReal-valued function of outcomes: discrete or continuous

2Types of events

Mutually exclusiveCannot occur together
ExhaustiveUnion is the whole sample space
Equally likelyEqual probability
Independent$P(A\cap B) = P(A)P(B)$
Complement $A^c$All outcomes outside $A$
Impossible / certain$P = 0$ / $P = 1$

3Three approaches

ApproachDefinition
Classical (mathematical)$P(E) = \dfrac{m}{n}$: favourable ÷ total, for exhaustive, mutually exclusive, equally likely outcomes
Statistical (relative frequency)$P(E) = \lim\limits_{n\to\infty} \dfrac{m}{n}$ from repeated trials
Axiomatic (Kolmogorov)(1) $P(E) \ge 0$ · (2) $P(S) = 1$ · (3) additivity for mutually exclusive events

Always $0 \le P(E) \le 1$ and $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A)$.

4Set notation

$A\cup B$$A$ or $B$ or both
$A\cap B$Both $A$ and $B$
$A^c$Not $A$
$A\mid B$$A$ given $B$

5Addition law

Any two events$$P(A\cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A\cap B)$$
Mutually exclusive$$P(A\cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$$

6Conditional & multiplication

Conditional, $P(B) > 0$$$P(A\mid B) = \frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$$
General$$P(A\cap B) = P(A)P(B\mid A)$$
Independent$$P(A\cap B) = P(A)P(B)$$

With replacement: draws independent. Without replacement: generally dependent.

7Bayes' theorem

Gives the probability of a cause given the observed evidence.

Two events$$P(A\mid B) = \frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$
Partition $E_1,\ldots,E_n$$$P(E_i\mid A) = \frac{P(E_i)P(A\mid E_i)}{\sum_k P(E_k)P(A\mid E_k)}$$

Prior

Probability before the evidence: $P(E_i)$

Posterior

Updated probability after the evidence: $P(E_i\mid A)$

8Counting

Combinations (order ignored)$${}^nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$$

Gambler's fallacy: in independent trials, past outcomes do not change the next probability.

!Common mistakes

  • Mutually exclusive ≠ independent: exclusive events with non-zero probabilities are always dependent.
  • Forgetting to subtract $P(A\cap B)$ for events that can occur together · Confusing $P(A\mid B)$ with $P(B\mid A)$.
