1Why design experiments?

Design of Experiments (DoE): planning experiments so treatment effects are estimated accurately, efficiently and without bias.

Objectives: validity · reliability · replicability · optimality.

A good design gives unbiased estimates with their uncertainty, detects important differences, includes the analysis plan, is easy to interpret, has wide validity, uses minimal resources and is as simple as possible.

2Key terms

TreatmentAny object of comparison
ControlStandard treatment for comparison
Experimental unitSmallest unit to which a treatment is independently applied
Observational unitUnit on which the response is measured
ResponseMeasured outcome
Factor / levelsVariable studied / its settings

3Experimental error

Variation among experimental units receiving the same treatment, caused by extraneous factors.

Every design must provide a way to estimate it.

4Fisher's three basic principles

PrincipleWhat it meansWhy
RandomizationTreatments assigned to units purely by chanceProtects against allocation bias; balances unknown sources of variation on average
ReplicationSame treatment on more than one unitEstimates experimental error; improves precision and reliability
Local control (blocking)Group similar units into blocks before randomisingReduces experimental error from known variation

In a Randomized Block Design, all treatments are randomly assigned within each block of similar units.

5Improving precision

  • Border effect: neighbouring plots affect border plants, so record only the inner net plot.
  • Proper plot technique: keep everything except the treatment uniform.
  • Appropriate analysis: e.g. ANCOVA adjusts for covariates.
  • Planning and recording: block for time or batches; keep original records.
  • Matching the design to the experimental units.

6History

  • R.A. Fisher founded the statistical principles of DoE at Rothamsted Experimental Station in the 1920s–30s.
  • He introduced ANOVA to partition variation into factors and chance.
  • With Frank Yates, he built methods still central to agricultural research.
  • Fisher's tea-tasting experiment shows why randomization matters.

7Example: pot experiment with manures

TreatmentsPoultry manure, cow dung, coir-pith compost
Experimental unitOne pot receiving one manure
ReplicationSeveral pots per manure
ResponseYield per plant
ErrorYield differences among pots given the same manure

!Common mistakes

  • Pseudo-replication: counting several plants in one pot as replicates.
  • Randomizing the field but always applying treatments in the same order.
  • Recording border plants.
