Regression Analysis

Fitting the least-squares line, regression coefficients, the two regression lines and model assumptions.
5-minute revision

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Regression predicts a response from an explanatory variable.

RememberThe slope gives the change in predicted response for one unit of the predictor. A fitted line does not establish causation.

Apply itIf predicted yield = 2 + 0.04 × nitrogen, adding 10 units of nitrogen changes predicted yield by 0.4 units within the studied range.

1 minute: key idea · 3 minutes: methods & examples · 1 minute: test yourself

1The model

Regression models how a dependent (response) variable depends on one or more independent (predictor) variables.

Simple linear regression$$y_i = \alpha + \beta x_i + e_i$$

Error $e_i$

Theoretical deviation from the true line

Residual $\hat{e}_i$

Observed − fitted: $y_i - (\hat\alpha + \hat\beta x_i)$

2Slope & intercept

  • Slope $\beta$: expected change in $y$ for a one-unit increase in $x$. Range $-\infty$ to $+\infty$; $+$ direct, $-$ inverse.
  • Intercept $\alpha$: value of $y$ when $x = 0$.
Simple1 predictor
Multiple2 or more: $y = \alpha + \beta_1 x_1 + \cdots + \beta_k x_k + e$
LogisticCategorical response

3Least squares

Choose $\alpha, \beta$ to minimise $E = \sum [y_i - (\alpha + \beta x_i)]^2$. This gives the normal equations:

First$$\textstyle\sum y = n\alpha + \beta \sum x$$
Second$$\textstyle\sum xy = \alpha \sum x + \beta \sum x^2$$
Slope$$\hat\beta_{yx} = \frac{\sum xy - \frac{\sum x \sum y}{n}}{\sum x^2 - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{n}}$$
Intercept$$\hat\alpha = \bar{y} - \hat\beta \bar{x}$$

Equivalently $\hat\beta_{yx} = cov(x,y)/var(x)$. The fitted line passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$.

4Two regression lines

$y$ on $x$ (predict $y$)$$\beta_{yx} = r\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$$
$x$ on $y$ (predict $x$)$$\beta_{xy} = r\frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}$$
Link to correlation$$\beta_{yx}\,\beta_{xy} = r^2$$

5Properties of regression coefficients

  • Both have the same sign, and $r$ takes that sign: $r = \pm\sqrt{\beta_{yx}\beta_{xy}}$.
  • If one exceeds 1, the other must be less than 1.
  • $r = 0$: both coefficients are zero.
  • $r = \pm 1$: the two lines coincide.
  • Independent of change of origin, not of scale: with $X=\frac{x-a}{h}$, $Y=\frac{y-b}{k}$, $\beta_{YX} = \frac{h}{k}\beta_{yx}$.

6Assumptions

  • Linearity of the mean relationship
  • Independence of errors (no autocorrelation)
  • Constant error variance: homoscedasticity
  • Normality of errors, for exact inference
  • No exact multicollinearity among predictors
  • Correct model specification

Check the fitted model with residual plots.

7Correlation vs regression

CorrelationRegression
PurposeMeasures associationModels the relationship and predicts
SymmetrySymmetric: $r_{xy} = r_{yx}$Asymmetric: $y$ on $x$ ≠ $x$ on $y$
UnitsUnit-freeCoefficients carry units
Range−1 to +1Unbounded

!Common mistakes

  • Using the $y$-on-$x$ line to predict $x$.
  • Coefficients of opposite signs (impossible).
  • Treating a regression fit as proof of cause.
  • Predicting far outside the data range.
