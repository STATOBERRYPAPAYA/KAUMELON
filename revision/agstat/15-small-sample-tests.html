Small Sample Tests

Student's t tests, the correlation test, chi-square tests and the F test for variances.
5-minute revision
Textbook authors: Dr. Pratheesh P. Gopinath · Dr. Adarsh V.S. · Mohammed Hisham M. · Dr. Manju Mary Paul

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Choose the test from the study structure.

RememberPaired data require analysis of within-pair differences. Independent groups require an independent-samples method.

Apply itBefore-and-after measurements on the same plants are paired; they are not two independent samples.

1 minute: key idea · 3 minutes: methods & examples · 1 minute: test yourself

1Small samples

Often $n < 30$ (not a strict boundary). Uses the exact $t$, $\chi^2$ and $F$ distributions.

W.S. Gosset published the $t$ distribution as "Student". It has heavier tails than the normal.

2Student's $t$ tests for means

TestStatisticdf
One sample, $H_0: \mu = \mu_0$$t = \dfrac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}$$n - 1$
Two samples, equal variances$t = \dfrac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}}$, pooled $s^2 = \dfrac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}$$n_1 + n_2 - 2$
Welch (unequal variances)$t = \dfrac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$Welch–Satterthwaite (not an integer)
Paired: before/after on the same unit$t = \dfrac{\bar{d}}{s_d/\sqrt{n}}$, $d_i = x_i - y_i$$n - 1$

3Testing a correlation

$H_0: \rho = 0$, df $= n - 2$$$t = \frac{r\sqrt{n - 2}}{\sqrt{1 - r^2}}$$

Assumes a bivariate normal model.

4$F$ test: two variances

$H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$$$F = \frac{s_1^2}{s_2^2},\quad df = (n_1 - 1,\ n_2 - 1)$$

Put the larger variance on top ($F \ge 1$) and use the upper $\alpha/2$ cut-off for a two-sided test.

5Chi-square tests

TestStatisticdf
Goodness of fit$\chi^2 = \sum \dfrac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}$$n - 1 - m$
Independence ($r \times s$ table)$\chi^2 = \sum\sum \dfrac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}$, $E_{ij} = \dfrac{A_i B_j}{N}$$(r-1)(s-1)$
2 × 2 table$\chi^2 = \dfrac{n(ad - bc)^2}{C_1 C_2 R_1 R_2}$1
Population variance$\chi^2 = \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2}$$n - 1$

$n$ categories, $m$ parameters estimated from the data. Expected = row total × column total ÷ grand total. Goodness-of-fit and independence use the upper tail.

6Yates' correction

Optional continuity adjustment for 2 × 2 tables:

Corrected$$\frac{n[\max(0, \lvert ad - bc\rvert - \frac{n}{2})]^2}{C_1C_2R_1R_2}$$

With small expected counts, consider an exact test.

7Which test?

QuestionTest
Does mean plant height equal 90 cm?One-sample $t$
Do two independent varieties differ in yield?Two-sample $t$ (pooled or Welch)
Did yield change on the same plots before and after a treatment?Paired $t$
Do segregation counts fit a 9:3:3:1 ratio?$\chi^2$ goodness of fit
Is adoption independent of education level?$\chi^2$ independence
Are two varieties equally variable?$F$ test

!Common mistakes

  • Two-sample $t$ on paired data.
  • Wrong df: $n-1$ vs $n_1+n_2-2$ vs $(r-1)(s-1)$.
  • Running $\chi^2$ on percentages instead of counts.
