Sample Survey

Survey terminology, sampling and non-sampling errors, confidence intervals, and probability and non-probability sampling methods.

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Sampling design determines how far results can be generalised.

RememberRandom sampling controls selection; stratification samples within groups; clustering samples groups of units.

Apply itTo represent both irrigated and rainfed farms, stratify by irrigation status and randomly sample within each stratum.

1 minute: key idea · 3 minutes: methods & examples · 1 minute: test yourself

1Key terms

CensusComplete enumeration of every unit
Sampling unitBasic unit selected
Sampling frameComplete list identifying the units
Sampling designProcedure used to select the sample
Estimator / estimateStatistic used for a parameter / its particular value
Unbiased$E(t) = \theta$; e.g. $E(\bar{X}) = \mu$
Sampling fraction$$f = \frac{n}{N}$$

2Errors in surveys

Sampling error

Arises from observing a sample, not the whole population. Absent in a census. Larger $n$ generally reduces standard error.

Non-sampling error

Everything else: non-response, measurement, recording, processing. Present even in a census.

Reliable results need good design, representative sampling, trained enumerators and careful execution.

3Confidence interval

An interval estimate of a parameter.

Confidence level$$100(1 - \alpha)\%$$

$\alpha = 0.05$ → 95%; $\alpha = 0.01$ → 99%. "95%" describes the long-run performance of the method.

4Probability sampling methods

MethodHowBest when
Simple random (SRSWR / SRSWOR)Equal inclusion probabilities, with or without replacementComplete frame, fairly homogeneous population
StratifiedSplit into non-overlapping strata; sample each independentlyStrata internally homogeneous: better precision
SystematicRandom start, then every $k$th unit; $k = N/n$Ordered lists, field rows
ClusterSelect clusters at random; enumerate all units in themNo unit-level frame; dispersed population
MultistageSample in stages (e.g. village → field → plot)Large-scale surveys such as GCES

Probability sampling allows probability-based inference and measurable sampling error.

5Non-probability sampling

  • Convenience: easily accessible units
  • Consecutive: every accessible eligible unit in turn
  • Quota: fill subgroup quotas non-randomly
  • Purposive (judgement): deliberately chosen informative units
  • Snowball: participants refer others

Useful without a frame or for hard-to-reach groups, but sampling error cannot be measured by probability theory.

6Survey steps & pioneers

Principal steps: objectives → target population → frame and units → data needed → questionnaire → collection method → pilot survey → design and sample size → fieldwork (handle non-response) → edit, tabulate, analyse → report → document.

!Common mistakes

  • Calling a convenience sample "random" · A census still has non-sampling errors · SRS draws are independent only with replacement · Stratified (sample within every group) ≠ cluster (take whole groups).
