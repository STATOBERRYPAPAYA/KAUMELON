Central Tendency I

Simple averages: the arithmetic mean, the median and the mode.

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Choose the average for the question.

RememberMean uses every value; median describes the middle; mode identifies the most frequent value or category.

Apply itFor yields 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, the mean is 6 but the median is 3. The unusually large yield pulls the mean upwards.

1The idea · types of averages

A measure of central tendency is a single value that represents the centre or typical value of a dataset.

Simple averages
this chapter		Mean · Median · Mode
Special averagesGeometric mean · Harmonic mean
Positional averagesQuartiles · Deciles · Percentiles

Objectives: condense data into a single value; make comparisons easy.

2A good average should be…

  • Rigidly defined
  • Simple to understand and easy to calculate
  • Based on all values of the data
  • Capable of further mathematical treatment
  • Stable from sample to sample (sampling stability)
  • Not unduly affected by extreme values

These are desirable properties, not a universal checklist. The appropriate average depends on the data and the purpose.

3Arithmetic mean

The sum of the observations divided by their number. It is what people usually mean by "average".

Raw data$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}$$
Frequency distribution$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{\sum f_i}$$
Assumed mean, $d_i = x_i - A$$$\bar{x} = A + \frac{\sum f_i d_i}{\sum f_i}$$
Coding, $u_i = (x_i - A)/C$$$\bar{x} = A + C\,\frac{\sum f_i u_i}{\sum f_i}$$
Weighted mean$$\bar{x}_w = \frac{\sum w_i x_i}{\sum w_i}$$
Combined mean of groups$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum n_j \bar{x}_j}{\sum n_j}$$

For grouped data, $x_i$ is the class mark, so the mean is an approximation. With equal weights, the weighted mean is the simple mean.

4Properties of the mean

Deviations sum to zero$$\sum (x_i - \bar{x}) = 0$$
Least squares$$\sum (x_i - a)^2 \text{ is least at } a = \bar{x}$$
Change of origin & scale$$y_i = a + b x_i \Rightarrow \bar{y} = a + b\bar{x}$$

Combine group means by weighting with group sizes, not by averaging the means.

5Median

The middle value after arranging the data in ascending order. It is a positional average.

Odd $n$

$\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}$ observation

Even $n$

mean of the $\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}$ and $\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}$ observations
Grouped data (linear interpolation)$$Md = L + \left(\frac{\frac{N}{2} - F}{f_m}\right) C$$

Median class holds the $(N/2)^{\text{th}}$ value · $L$ its lower boundary · $F$ cumulative frequency below $L$ · $f_m$ its frequency · $C$ its width. Graphically, read it off the less-than ogive at $N/2$.

6Mode

The value that occurs most often. Found by inspection for raw data. It is the right average for qualitative (nominal) data.

Grouped data (equal class widths)$$Mo = L + \left(\frac{f_m - f_p}{2f_m - f_p - f_s}\right) C$$

Modal class has the highest frequency · $L$ its lower boundary · $f_p$, $f_s$ frequencies of the preceding and succeeding classes · $C$ class width. Graphically, use the histogram.

With unequal widths, compare frequency densities rather than raw frequencies. Data can be bimodal or have no mode.

8Shape & order

  • Symmetric, unimodal: mean = median = mode
  • Many positively skewed distributions: mean > median > mode
  • Many negatively skewed distributions: mean < median < mode

The skewed orderings are typical, not a universal theorem.

Positive observations (preview)$$AM \ge GM \ge HM$$

!Common mistakes

  • Averaging group means without weighting by group size.
  • Using class limits instead of boundaries for $L$.
  • Forgetting to sort the data before finding the median.
  • Applying the mode formula to unequal class widths.
