Factorial Experiments

1.Quick revision · 1

  • Factorial experiment → studies two or more factors simultaneously.
  • Treatment combinations → all possible combinations of factor levels.
  • Number of treatments → product of the number of levels of all factors.
  • Main effect → average change in response due to a change in the level of one factor.
  • Simple effect → effect of one factor at a particular level of another factor.

2.Quick revision · 2

  • Interaction → effect of one factor changes according to the level of another factor.
  • Parallel population mean profiles → no interaction on the response scale used.
  • Non-parallel sample mean profiles → an interaction pattern; an appropriate test is needed to assess statistical significance.
  • Symmetrical factorial → all factors have the same number of levels.
  • Asymmetrical factorial → factors have unequal numbers of levels.

3.Quick revision · 3

  • Complete factorial → all possible treatment combinations are included.
  • Incomplete or fractional factorial → only part of the possible combinations are included.
  • \(2^n\) factorial → \(n\) factors, each at two levels.
  • \(3^n\) factorial → \(n\) factors, each at three levels.
  • \(2^2\) → 4 treatment combinations.

4.Quick revision · 4

  • \(2^3\) → 8 treatment combinations.
  • \(2^4\) → 16 treatment combinations.
  • \(3^2\) → 9 treatment combinations.
  • \(3^3\) → 27 treatment combinations.
  • In a \(2^n\) factorial, the treatment degrees of freedom are \(2^n-1\).

5.Quick revision · 5

  • In a \(3^n\) factorial, the treatment degrees of freedom are \(3^n-1\).
  • In a \(2^2\) factorial → A, B, and \(A\times B\) each have 1 degree of freedom.
  • In a \(2^3\) factorial → A, B, C, \(A\times B\), \(A\times C\), \(B\times C\), and \(A\times B\times C\) each have 1 degree of freedom.
  • In a \(3^2\) factorial → A has 2 df, B has 2 df, and \(A\times B\) has 4 df.
  • For a three-level factor → 2 df can be divided into linear and quadratic components.

6.Quick revision · 6

  • Yates’s method → tabular method for calculating effects and sums of squares in \(2^n\) factorial experiments.
  • Fisher’s algebraic method → expresses factorial effects and interactions algebraically.
  • Confounding → deliberately making an interaction inseparable from block effects to reduce block size.
  • Complete confounding → the same interaction is confounded in all replications.
  • Partial confounding → different interactions are confounded in different replications.

7.Quick revision · 7

  • Higher-order interactions are generally preferred for confounding because main effects and lower-order interactions are usually more important.
  • Major advantage of confounding → smaller and more homogeneous blocks, resulting in improved precision.
  • Major disadvantage of confounding → loss of information about the confounded interactions.

8.Important formulae · 1

Number of treatment combinations in a factorial experiment:

\[ N=\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_i \] {#eq-ntreat}

Number of treatment combinations in a \(2^n\) factorial experiment:

\[ N=2^n \] {#eq-n2n}

Number of treatment combinations in a \(3^n\) factorial experiment:

\[ N=3^n \] {#eq-n3n}

9.Important formulae · 2

Main effect of A in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ \text{Main effect of A}=\frac{(A)_{i=1}-(A)_{i=0}}{2r} \] {#eq-maineffA22}

Sum of squares due to A in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ SS_A=\frac{[(A)_{i=1}-(A)_{i=0}]^2}{4r} \] {#eq-ssA22}

Main effect of B in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ \text{Main effect of B}=\frac{(B)_{j=1}-(B)_{j=0}}{2r} \] {#eq-maineffB22}

10.Important formulae · 3

Sum of squares due to B in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ SS_B=\frac{[(B)_{j=1}-(B)_{j=0}]^2}{4r} \] {#eq-ssB22}

Interaction effect of \(A \times B\) in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ \text{Interaction effect of }A\times B=\frac{(AB)_{i+j=0}-(AB)_{i+j=1}}{2r} \] {#eq-intAB22}

Sum of squares due to \(A \times B\):

\[ SS_{AB}=\frac{[(AB)_{i+j=0}-(AB)_{i+j=1}]^2}{4r} \] {#eq-ssAB22}

11.Important formulae · 4

Critical difference for a significant interaction in a \(2^2\) factorial experiment:

\[ CD=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{r}} \] {#eq-cd22int}

Main effect of A in a \(2^3\) factorial experiment:

\[ \text{Main effect of A}=\frac{(A)_{i=1}-(A)_{i=0}}{4r} \] {#eq-maineffA23}

Sum of squares due to A in a \(2^3\) factorial experiment:

\[ SS_A=\frac{[(A)_{i=1}-(A)_{i=0}]^2}{8r} \] {#eq-ssA23}

12.Important formulae · 5

Interaction effect of \(B\times C\) in a \(2^3\) factorial experiment:

\[ \text{Interaction effect of }B\times C=\frac{(BC)_{j+k=0}-(BC)_{j+k=1}}{4r} \] {#eq-intBC23}

Sum of squares due to \(B\times C\):

\[ SS_{BC}=\frac{[(BC)_{j+k=0}-(BC)_{j+k=1}]^2}{8r} \] {#eq-ssBC23}

Interaction effect of \(A\times B\times C\):

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{Interaction effect of }A\times B\times C\\ =\frac{(ABC)_{i+j+k=1}-(ABC)_{i+j+k=0}}{4r} \end{gathered} \] {#eq-intABC}

13.Important formulae · 6

Sum of squares due to \(A\times B\times C\):

\[ SS_{ABC}=\frac{[(ABC)_{i+j+k=1}-(ABC)_{i+j+k=0}]^2}{8r} \] {#eq-ssABC}

Critical difference for comparing means of a two-factor interaction in a \(2^3\) factorial:

\[ CD=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{MSE}{r}} \] {#eq-cd23twofactor}

Critical difference for comparing means when the three-factor interaction is significant:

\[ CD=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{r}} \] {#eq-cd23threefactor}

14.Important formulae · 7

Linear comparison for a factor at three levels:

\[ A_L=(A)_{i=2}-(A)_{i=0} \] {#eq-linearcomp}

Quadratic comparison for a factor at three levels:

\[ A_Q=(A)_{i=2}+(A)_{i=0}-2(A)_{i=1} \] {#eq-quadcomp}

Sum of squares due to A in a \(3^2\) factorial:

\[ SS_A=\frac{(A)_{i=0}^2+(A)_{i=1}^2+(A)_{i=2}^2}{3r}-CF \] {#eq-ssA32}

15.Important formulae · 8

Sum of squares due to B in a \(3^2\) factorial:

\[ SS_B=\frac{(B)_{j=0}^2+(B)_{j=1}^2+(B)_{j=2}^2}{3r}-CF \] {#eq-ssB32}

Sum of squares due to \(A\times B\) in a \(3^2\) factorial:

\[ SS_{AB}=SS_{\text{two-way table}}-(SS_A+SS_B) \] {#eq-ssAB32}

Critical difference for comparing levels of A in a \(3^2\) factorial:

\[ CD=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{3r}} \] {#eq-cdA32}

16.Important formulae · 9

Critical difference for comparing levels of A in a \(2\times3\) factorial:

\[ CD_A=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{3r}} \] {#eq-cdA23asym}

Critical difference for comparing levels of B in a \(2\times3\) factorial:

\[ CD_B=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{2r}} \] {#eq-cdB23asym}

Critical difference for comparing means when \(A\times B\) is significant:

\[ CD=t_{\alpha/2,edf}\sqrt{\frac{2MSE}{r}} \] {#eq-cdAB23asym}

17.Self-check from the chapter

  1. An experiment containing all combinations of the levels of its factors is called a __________ factorial experiment.

    Full

  2. The number of treatment combinations is the __________ of the numbers of levels of the factors.

    Product

  3. The effect of a factor averaged over the levels of the other factors is called its __________ effect.

    Main

  4. The effect of one factor at a specified level of another is called a __________ effect.

    Simple
