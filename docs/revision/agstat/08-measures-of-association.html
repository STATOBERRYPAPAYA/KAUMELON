Measures of Association

Scatter diagrams, covariance, Pearson's and Spearman's correlation, Kendall's concordance and correlation matrices.

1Relationships & the scatter diagram

Linear

Can be represented by a straight line.

Monotonic

One variable consistently rises (or falls) as the other rises, not necessarily at a constant rate.

A scatter diagram plots paired observations $(x_i, y_i)$: always look at it before computing a coefficient.

3Karl Pearson's correlation coefficient $r$

Covariance$$cov(x,y) = \frac{1}{n}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})$$
Correlation$$r = \frac{cov(x,y)}{sd(x)\,sd(y)}$$
Deviation form$$r = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}$$

Measures linear association. Covariance depends on units; $r$ is unit-free. $r$ is sensitive to outliers.

4Reading $r$

+1Perfect positive
near +1Strong positive
0No linear correlation
near −1Strong negative
−1Perfect negative

$-1 \le r \le +1$. Perfect: all points on a line.

5Spearman's rank correlation $\rho$

Computed from ranks; measures monotonic association. Good for ordinal data and curved but monotonic patterns. Range −1 to +1.

No tied ranks$$\rho = 1 - \frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}$$

$d_i$ = difference between the ranks of the $i$th pair. Ties get their average rank and correction terms $T = \frac{1}{12}\sum m(m^2-1)$.

6Kendall's coefficient of concordance $W$

Measures agreement among several judges' rankings (e.g. $k$ judges ranking $n$ varieties).

Concordance$$W = \frac{12\left[\sum R_i^2 - \frac{(\sum R_i)^2}{n}\right]}{k^2 n(n^2 - 1)}$$

$R_i$ = sum of ranks for object $i$; $k$ = number of judges.

7Correlation matrix & correlogram

Correlation matrix

Table of pairwise correlations among several variables. Diagonal elements = 1; symmetric about the diagonal.

Correlogram

A graphical display of the correlation matrix (colour or size shows strength and sign).

!Common mistakes

  • Correlation is not causation.
  • $r = 0$ rules out only linear association; a curve can still exist.
  • Skipping the scatter plot.
  • Forgetting average ranks for ties.
