Measures of Dispersion

Range, interquartile range, mean absolute deviation, variance, standard deviation and the coefficient of variation.
5-minute revision
Textbook authors: Dr. Pratheesh P. Gopinath · Dr. Adarsh V.S. · Mohammed Hisham M. · Dr. Manju Mary Paul

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Report spread alongside the average.

RememberVariance has squared units; standard deviation has the original units. CV is useful only when a meaningful positive mean exists.

Apply itTwo crops can have the same mean yield and different variability. A smaller SD indicates more consistent yields on the same scale.

1 minute: key idea · 3 minutes: methods & examples · 1 minute: test yourself

1What is dispersion?

The extent to which observations deviate from a central value: the spread of the data.

Absolute measures

In the data's units: range, IQR, MAD, SD

Relative measures

Unit-free: coefficient of variation (CV)

A good measure is rigidly defined, easy to compute, based on all values, suitable for further algebra and not unduly affected by extremes.

2Range & IQR

Range$$R = L - S$$
Interquartile range$$IQR = Q_3 - Q_1$$
  • Range: simplest; uses only the two extremes.
  • IQR: spread of the middle 50%; less affected by extremes.
  • Quartile deviation = IQR ÷ 2.

3Mean absolute deviation

MAD about the mean$$MAD = \frac{\sum \lvert x_i - \bar{x} \rvert}{n}$$

Uses all observations but ignores signs. MAD is least about the median.

4Variance & standard deviation

Sample variance$$s^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n-1}$$
Shortcut$$s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\left\{\sum x_i^2 - \frac{(\sum x_i)^2}{n}\right\}$$
Discrete frequency$$s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\left\{\sum f_i x_i^2 - \frac{(\sum f_i x_i)^2}{n}\right\}$$
Grouped, $d_i = (x_i - A)/c$$$s = c\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\left\{\sum f_i d_i^2 - \frac{(\sum f_i d_i)^2}{n}\right\}}$$

SD $s$ = non-negative square root of $s^2$. Grouped data: $x_i$ = class mark, $A$ = class mark with the highest frequency, $c$ = class interval.

5Properties to remember

  • Variance is in squared units; SD is in the original units.
  • SD is always ≥ 0; SD = 0 means all observations are identical.
  • Sample variance uses divisor $n-1$ (unbiased).
  • Adding a constant to every value does not change SD.
  • Multiplying by $b$ multiplies SD by $\lvert b\rvert$.
  • Squaring gives extreme deviations more weight.

6Coefficient of variation

CV (%)$$CV = \frac{s}{\bar{x}} \times 100$$

A relative measure, used to compare variability of datasets in different units or with different means.

Higher CV

More relative variability: less consistent

Lower CV

Less relative variability: more consistent

7Comparing the measures

!Common mistakes

  • Dividing by $n$ for a sample variance.
  • Reporting variance in the original units.
  • Comparing SDs of data in different units (use CV).
  • Forgetting to multiply by $c$ in the grouped shortcut.
