1.Quick revision · 1

  • Uniformity trial → used to study the nature and extent of fertility variation in a field.
  • A single crop variety is grown over the entire field.
  • All management inputs, including fertilizer if used, are applied uniformly.
  • A substantial border is removed before dividing the field into basic units.
  • Basic unit → smallest unit from which yield is separately recorded.

2.Quick revision · 2

  • Yield differences among basic units → measure of soil heterogeneity.
  • Smaller basic units → finer spatial detail, balanced against harvesting accuracy and cost.
  • Uniformity trials can help determine → fertility variation, plot size, plot shape, and block size.
  • Fertility contour map → graphical representation of soil heterogeneity.
  • Moving averages → used to reduce random variation and identify areas of similar fertility.

3.Quick revision · 3

  • Similar fertility areas → grouped together in the contour map.
  • Serial correlation → measures the relationship between neighbouring observations.
  • Horizontal and vertical serial correlations → used to identify the direction of fertility variation.
  • Low serial correlation → little linear association at the chosen lag; this alone does not establish patches.
  • High serial correlation → neighbouring yields are associated; use the field map to assess gradients and patches.

4.Quick revision · 4

  • Mean square between strips → simpler method having the same objective as serial correlation.
  • Between vertical strips → measures variation across columns, in the horizontal direction.
  • Between horizontal strips → measures variation across rows, in the vertical direction.
  • Larger strip mean square → indicates greater variation in that direction.
  • Fairfield Smith’s variance law → relates plot size to the variance of yield per basic unit area.

5.Quick revision · 5

  • Smith’s law → \(V_x=V_1/x^b\).
  • Smith’s index of soil heterogeneity → \(b\).
  • \(b\) → quantitative measure of soil heterogeneity.
  • Lower \(b\) → slower fitted variance reduction, often associated with positive nearby correlation; a gradient is not established.
  • Higher \(b\) → a faster fitted reduction in variance as plot size increases; it does not establish a patch pattern.

6.Quick revision · 6

  • Values from 0 to 1 describe the usual idealised range of Smith’s index; empirical fitted values can fall outside this range.
  • Simulated plots → formed by combining basic units into different sizes and shapes.
  • Only plot arrangements that fit exactly within the whole field are used.
  • Between-plot sum of squares → calculated for each simulated plot size and shape.
  • Variance per basic unit area \(V_x\) → sample variance of plot means, equal to \(Q_x/(rc-x)\).

7.Quick revision · 7

  • Alternative shapes partition the same field → their variance estimates are dependent.
  • Retain shapes separately or state a descriptive averaging rule; an ordinary F test does not justify pooling dependent partitions.
  • Smith’s index → estimated by regression of \(Y=\log V_x-\log V_1\) on \(X=\log x\).
  • In \(Y=cX\), \(c=-b\).
  • Adjusted \(b\) → obtained using interpolation from the adjusted \(b\) table.

8.Quick revision · 8

  • Maximum curvature method → used to determine optimum plot size.
  • In maximum curvature method → plot size is plotted on the X-axis and CV on the Y-axis.
  • As plot size increases → CV generally decreases.
  • Apparent bend → a traditional plot-size guide sensitive to axis scales and the available arrangements.
  • In the rice example → the field was 12 m × 17 m.

9.Quick revision · 9

  • After removing a 1 m border from all sides → effective area was 10 m × 15 m.
  • With 1 m × 1 m basic units → there were 150 basic units.
  • In the example, vertical serial correlation → 0.438627.
  • In the example, horizontal serial correlation → 0.54317.
  • Positive sequence correlations describe nearby similarity; they do not by themselves establish patches or gradients.

10.Quick revision · 10

  • The higher horizontal sequence correlation alone does not establish a horizontal fertility gradient.
  • In the example, the larger horizontal-strip mean square indicates greater yield variation across rows; a monotonic gradient is not established.
  • For plot size 25 m² in the example → \(V_{25}=1750.14\).
  • The calculated Smith’s index in the example was \(b=0.3607\) before adjustment.
  • The adjusted Smith’s index in the example was approximately \(0.41156\).

11.Quick revision · 11

  • The index summarises variance reduction with plot size; identifying gradients or patches also requires spatial information.
  • Uniformity trials are important because field variability exists even when all management practices are kept uniform.

12.Important formulae · 1

Serial correlation:

\[ r_s=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_iX_{i+1}-\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\right)^2}{n}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i^2-\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\right)^2}{n}} \]

Total number of basic units:

\[ n=r\times c \]

Vertical sum of squares:

\[ SS_V=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{c}V_i^2}{r}-\frac{G^2}{n} \]

13.Important formulae · 2

Horizontal sum of squares:

\[ SS_H=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}H_i^2}{c}-\frac{G^2}{n} \]

Vertical mean square:

\[ MS_V=\frac{SS_V}{c-1} \]

Horizontal mean square:

\[ MS_H=\frac{SS_H}{r-1} \]

14.Important formulae · 3

Fairfield Smith’s variance law:

\[ V_x=\frac{V_1}{x^b} \]

Logarithmic form of Smith’s variance law:

\[ \log V_x=\log V_1-b\log x \]

Difference form:

\[ \log V_x-\log V_1=-b\log x \]

15.Important formulae · 4

Regression form:

\[ Y=cX \]

where

\[ Y=\log V_x-\log V_1 \]

and

\[ X=\log x \]

Relationship between regression coefficient and Smith’s index:

\[ c=-b \]

16.Important formulae · 5

Number of simulated plots:

\[ w=\frac{rc}{x} \]

Between-plot sum of squares:

\[ Q_x=\sum_{i=1}^{w}\frac{T_i^2}{x}-\frac{G^2}{rc} \]

Variance per unit area:

\[ V_x=\frac{Q_x}{rc-x} \]

17.Important formulae · 6

Coefficient of variation:

\[ CV=\frac{\text{Standard deviation}}{\text{Mean}}\times100 \]

Standard deviation from variance:

\[ SD=\sqrt{V_x} \]

Adjusted Smith’s index:

\[ \text{Adjusted }b=y_1+(b_{\text{cal}}-L_1)\frac{y_2-y_1}{L_2-L_1} \]

18.Important formulae · 7

Descriptive ratio for two plot shapes (not an F test for dependent partitions):

\[ R=\frac{\text{Larger between-plot sum of squares}}{\text{Smaller between-plot sum of squares}} \]

19.Self-check from the chapter

  1. A field trial conducted under uniform treatment to study field variability is called a __________ trial.

    Uniformity

  2. The smallest harvested subdivision in a uniformity trial is called a __________ unit.

    Basic

  3. A map showing spatial patterns in yield is called a fertility __________ map.

    Contour

  4. The correlation between yields of neighbouring units is a form of __________ correlation.

    Serial
