Data Transformation

When ANOVA assumptions fail: log, square root, angular and Box-Cox transformations, and back-transformation.
5-minute revision
Textbook authors: Dr. Pratheesh P. Gopinath · Dr. Adarsh V.S. · Mohammed Hisham M. · Dr. Manju Mary Paul
STAT 3202KAU Me-LONChapter revisionKAU Me-LON · www.agrinoteskau.com · Dept. of Agricultural Statistics, College of Agriculture, Vellayani

THE IDEA TO KEEP

Transform to address a model problem, not to force significance.

RememberChoose a transformation from the data scale and residual behaviour. Document it and interpret results on the appropriate scale.

Apply itLog transformation turns multiplicative effects into additive ones, but log(0) is undefined. Any offset must be justified and reported.

1 minute: key idea · 3 minutes: methods & examples · 1 minute: test yourself

1Why transform?

Used when ANOVA assumptions fail, typically unequal error variances (heteroscedasticity), non-normal errors or non-additive (multiplicative) effects.

  1. Transform the data
  2. Run ANOVA and mean separation on the transformed scale
  3. Back-transform means to the original units for reporting

Never pick a transformation just to get the conclusion you want.

2Which transformation?

DataVarianceUse
Counts (Poisson)$\sigma^2 = \mu$ or $c^2\mu$Square root
Binomial percentages$\sigma^2 = p(1-p)/n$Angular (arcsine)
SD ∝ mean; multiplicative effects$\sigma^2 = c^2\mu^2$Logarithmic

3Logarithmic

Positive data$$X = \log Y$$
With zeros$$X = \log(Y + 1)$$

Back-transform: $Y = \text{antilog}\,X$. Logs need positive inputs. Example in the book: weed counts.

4Square root

Counts$$X = \sqrt{Y}$$
Zeros present$$X = \sqrt{Y + 1}$$

Very small counts: $\sqrt{Y} + \sqrt{Y+1}$. Back-transform $\sqrt{Y+1}$ with $Y = X^2 - 1$. Insect, larval, defective and diseased-seed counts.

5Angular (arcsine) transformation

Transform$$X = \sin^{-1}\sqrt{p},\quad p = \frac{Y}{100}$$
0% and 100%$$p = \frac{1}{4n},\quad p = 1 - \frac{1}{4n}$$
Back-transform$$Y = 100 \sin^2 X$$

For percentages from binomial counts (germination, mortality). The 30–70% guideline is approximate. Nutrient percentages (protein, carbohydrate) are not binomial and need not be transformed.

6Box-Cox

A family of power transformations indexed by $\lambda$, for strictly positive data.

$\lambda = \tfrac12$: square root · $\lambda = -1$: reciprocal · $\lambda \to 0$: log. The angular transformation is not in this family.

7After transforming

  • Check assumptions using the residuals of the fitted model.
  • Use one transformation consistently for the whole dataset.
  • CD on the transformed scale: $t_{\alpha/2,\,edf}\sqrt{2\,MSE/r}$ (RBD).
  • Back-transformed means are not arithmetic means (e.g. squared root means are ≤ the original mean).

!Common mistakes

  • Reporting transformed means as final results.
  • Taking log of zero.
  • Arcsine on non-binomial percentages.
