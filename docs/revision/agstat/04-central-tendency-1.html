1.Quick revision · 1

  • Mean → arithmetic average of observations.
  • Median → middle value after arranging observations.
  • Mode → most frequently occurring value.
  • Mean → uses all observations.
  • Median → based on position.

2.Quick revision · 2

  • Mode → based on frequency.
  • Mean is affected by extreme values.
  • Median is less affected by extreme values.
  • Mode is useful for identifying the most common category or value.
  • Mean + constant → mean increases by the same constant.

3.Quick revision · 3

  • Mean × constant → mean is multiplied by the same constant.
  • Adding a constant to every observation does not change the standard deviation.
  • Multiplying every observation by a constant multiplies the standard deviation by the absolute value of that constant.
  • Sum of deviations from the arithmetic mean = 0.
  • Standard deviation = 0 → all observations are identical.

4.Quick revision · 4

  • For positive observations, \(AM\geq GM\geq HM\).
  • Symmetric unimodal distribution with a finite mean and a central mode → mean = median = mode.
  • In many familiar positively skewed unimodal distributions, mean > median > mode; this is not a universal theorem.
  • In many familiar negatively skewed unimodal distributions, mean < median < mode; this is not a universal theorem.

5.Important formulae · 1

Arithmetic mean:

\[ \bar{x}=\frac{\sum x_i}{n} \]

Arithmetic mean for a frequency distribution:

\[ \bar{x}=\frac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i} \]

Assumed mean method:

\[ \bar{x}=A+\frac{\sum f_id_i}{\sum f_i} \]

where

\[ d_i=x_i-A \]

6.Important formulae · 2

Coding method:

\[ \bar{x}=A+C\frac{\sum f_iu_i}{\sum f_i} \]

where

\[ u_i=\frac{x_i-A}{C} \]

Weighted mean:

\[ \bar{x}_w=\frac{\sum w_ix_i}{\sum w_i} \]

Median for odd \(n\):

\[ Md=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ observation} \]

7.Important formulae · 3

Median for even \(n\):

\[ Md=\frac{1}{2} \left[ \left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ observation} + \left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ observation} \right] \]

Median for grouped data:

\[ Md=L+\left(\frac{\frac{N}{2}-F}{f}\right)C \]

Mode for grouped data:

\[ Mo=L+\left(\frac{f_m-f_p}{2f_m-f_p-f_s}\right)C \]

8.Important formulae · 4

Relationship among arithmetic mean, geometric mean, and harmonic mean:

\[ AM\geq GM\geq HM \]

9.Worked example from the chapter

Find the mean of the numbers 2, 4, 7, 8, 11, 12

Solution

\[\overline{x} = \frac{2 + 4 + 7 + 8 + 11 + 12}{6} = \frac{44}{6} = 7.33\]

10.Self-check from the chapter

  1. The sum of observations divided by their number is called the arithmetic __________.

    Mean

  2. The sum of deviations from the arithmetic mean is __________.

    Zero

  3. The average calculated using assigned weights is called the __________ mean.

    Weighted

  4. The middle value of an ordered dataset is called the __________.

    Median
